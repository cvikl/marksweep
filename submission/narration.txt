1. Title
An investigator comes back from a Hong Kong market with two hundred photos. Each one needs a note, a score and a link to the register, and someone types them by hand. Nothing records which mark was compared with which, or why. Google Lens tells you what a logo is. Marksweep tells a lawyer why it might be infringing: a score, a reason, and a chain of evidence. A photo from the field in, a trademark evidence record out.

2. Upload
We are acting for a brand owner. The investigator picks the photo, picks the client, writes two lines of field notes: what goods, which jurisdiction. Press run.

3. Segment
First, SAM 3.1 is asked three plain questions through the Meta Model API: where is a brand logo, where is a printed brand name, where is a repeating pattern. Every answer comes back as a box and a lossless mask with a stable object id. Boxes are merged, cut from the original photo, and each crop is hashed. No person decided what to look at.

4. Assess
Then each crop goes to Muse Spark, next to the official register drawings of the client's marks. It returns structured JSON only: what it sees, the text it reads, which registrations are candidates, and six scores with one-sentence reasons. Visual, aural, conceptual, goods, distinctiveness, imitation signals. The same six questions a court asks.

5. Record
The record. The photo with numbered, colour-banded boxes. Red is highly concerning, amber worth checking, green low concern. Every element has its crop, its score and its rationales. Click a candidate mark and you land on the office record. Every number is a research signal for the lawyer; the tool never says infringes.

6. Control
A control: another house's monogram, for the same client. Nothing matches, every score is capped at twenty five, and the narrative says so. The model is not rewarded for finding trouble.

7. Ledger
Under every record, the ledger: every model call, with model id, purpose, latency and tokens. Four photos, thirty-four model calls, three and a half minutes end to end. The same code also runs Meta's open-weight Muse Glimmer on a laptop CPU, for firms whose photos must never leave the building; slower, but no cloud at all.

8. End
Marksweep is the field-intake module of Signum, our case assessment product piloting with a Hong Kong IP firm.
